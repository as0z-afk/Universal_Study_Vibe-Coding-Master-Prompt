    How the course fits together

    Three passes over the same subject: first the language, then the physical channel, then the state of the whole system.

    Foundations · 1–2

    Describe and predict

    Classify a signal, split it into even and odd parts, then use the impulse response to predict the output of any linear time-invariant system through convolution.

    Transforms · 5–7

    Change the point of view

    Fourier turns time into frequency, Laplace adds the complex plane, and the z-transform does the same for discrete time. Poles and zeros tell you how a system will behave before you solve it.

    Communication · 3–4, 8

    Move information

    Waves carry data, modulation places it on a carrier, sampling turns it into numbers, and the Nyquist and Shannon limits decide how fast it can travel.

    Systems · 9

    See the state

    State-space analysis writes any order of differential equation as a first-order vector system, exposing stability, modes and controllability at a glance.

    Where the material comes from

    The course is built from the supplied ETE225 lecture material — Waves and Signals, Analog Transmission, and the Forouzan Data and Signals chapter — together with the full chapter structure of Hwei P. Hsu's Schaum's Outline of Signals and Systems. Examples labelled illustrative are constructed for teaching, not attributed to either source. Where a slide leaves a derivation implicit, the reasoning is shown in full and the assumption is stated.
